PPSU is polyphenylsulfone. Makers sell it for aerospace, automotive, dental and medical uses, and cite its heat and chemical resistance. Its glass transition is typically 220 °C. It lies beyond the H2C: makers ask for 360⁠–⁠400 °C at the nozzle, a bed hotter than the H2C reaches and a heated chamber above its limit, though a maker says a standard nozzle suffices.

Partly documented Tensile modulus (XY), tensile strength (XY) and HDT at 0.45 MPa rest on 1 product each and tensile strength (Z), notched Charpy impact and price are not published. Each figure is the median of the products that publish it comparably, and beside it the middle half of them; a value on a test bar no document describes as printed, or with no stated test load, is set apart, except a heat deflection of a plastic that cannot crystallise, which its glass transition sets, unless its document points to a moulded or resin bar.

Among the materials

Among the materials, PPSU has a high HDT at 0.45 MPa and density, and a low elongation at break (XY): each is in the top or bottom quarter of the materials that publish it. Its tensile modulus (XY) and tensile strength (XY) sit in the middle half.

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PPSU typically reaches a tensile strength of 55 MPa at a modulus of 2.31 GPa, and breaks at 3 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 110 MPa at a flexural modulus of 2.21 GPa.

What it is sold for

  • It also is designed to meet the UL 94 V-0 standards with low smoke evolution and low smoke toxicity, making the filament extremely suitable for automotive applications. FormFutura PPSU, data sheet, p. 1

Density and price
