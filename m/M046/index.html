TPC / TPEE is a thermoplastic copolyester elastomer. Makers sell it for parts that bend continuously and for soft parts in fashion and healthcare, and its glass transition is -35 °C. On the H2C most products print at 220 °C without chamber heat. Makers warn that it needs a direct-drive extruder and a low print speed, and that it can shed a little powder.

Partly documented Tensile modulus (XY), tensile strength (XY) and price rest on 2 products each and tensile strength (Z) and notched Charpy impact are not published. Each figure is the median of the products that publish it comparably, and beside it the middle half of them; a value on a test bar no document describes as printed, or with no stated test load, is set apart, except a heat deflection of a plastic that cannot crystallise, which its glass transition sets, unless its document points to a moulded or resin bar. Products with an added filler or additive are shown but kept out of the medians.

Among the materials

Against TPU 95A class, TPC / TPEE is 59 % weaker flat, 78 % stiffer and 63 % more expensive. Against TPU 85A class and softer, TPC / TPEE is 3.8 times as stiff, 42 % weaker flat and 52 % more expensive. Among the materials, TPC / TPEE has a high elongation at break (XY) and a low tensile modulus (XY) and tensile strength (XY): each is in the top or bottom quarter of the materials that publish it. Its density and price sit in the middle half.

What makers say about printing it

Printing advice

  • It is recommended to lower the print speed and reduce the extruder pressure to ensure a stable, high-quality print. Fiberlogy FiberFlex 30D, maker's product page or guide, p. 1

Moisture sensitivity

  • Drying conditions: 60°C / 4 hours Fiberlogy FiberFlex 30D, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
More from makers: warping and shrinkage, nozzle wear and adhesion between layers

Warping and shrinkage

  • Warping: Corners of the print lift and detach from the platform. Advice is to increase the build plate temperature. Wait long enough to allow the heat to dissipate to the top surface of the substrate. DSM Arnitel ID 2045, maker's product page or guide, p. 3

Nozzle wear

  • Note that for different nozzle diameters the settings should be changed accordingly. DSM Arnitel ID 2045, maker's product page or guide, p. 2

Adhesion between layers

  • The material delivers excellent adhesion in the Z-direction, meaning that the printed layers don’t detach - even with extreme deformation. BASF Forward AM Ultrafuse TPC 45D, data sheet, p. 1

Uses, limitations and chemicals

What it is sold for

  • The TPC is used in dynamic applications where prolonged constant flexibility is required, such as applications where material is continuously bent becomes. Fabru Thermoplastic polyester elastomer (TPC), data sheet, p. 1
  • With Chinchilla, you can create soft, long-lasting printed parts for fashion, healthcare and more. NinjaTek Chinchilla, data sheet, p. 1
  • Orthopedics and rehabilitation: orthoses, orthopedic insoles Fiberlogy FiberFlex Aero, maker's product page or guide, p. 1

What makers warn about

  • Only printable with Direct Drive Extruder - low printing speed Fabru Thermoplastic polyester elastomer (TPC), data sheet, p. 1
  • However, printing with FiberFlex 30D filament requires some experience and a printer equipped with a direct-drive extruder, due to the material’s softness, which can pose challenges during filament feeding. Fiberlogy FiberFlex 30D, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
Finish and accuracy
  • Chinchilla™ flexible 3D printer filament is the softest, best-feeling matte material in the market today. NinjaTek Chinchilla, data sheet, p. 1
  • Fiberlogy MattFlex 40D is a modern, flexible 3D printing filament that stands out for its matte finish and rubber-like properties. Fiberlogy MattFlex 40D, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
