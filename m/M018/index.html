PLA-CF is carbon-fibre-filled PLA. Makers sell it for functional parts, design projects and everyday prototyping, and describe a matt surface with a carbon-fibre look. It is amorphous as printed, so it softens at its glass transition: its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 55 °C. On the H2C every product needs a hardened nozzle, but it prints at 200⁠–⁠230 °C with no heated chamber.

Among the materials

Carbon fibre adds little to PLA's heat resistance: PLA-CF's HDT at 0.45 MPa is 55 °C against 56 °C. Against PETG-CF, PLA-CF is 24 % less stiff and 24 % less tough in notched impact, and its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 17 °C lower. Among the materials, PLA-CF has a low HDT at 0.45 MPa and price: both are in the lowest quarter of the materials that publish them. The rest of its key properties sit in the middle half.

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PLA-CF typically reaches a tensile strength of 42.3 MPa at a modulus of 2.8 GPa, and breaks at 6.65 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 85 MPa at a flexural modulus of 4.07 GPa.

Thermal properties

PLA-CF is amorphous as printed, so it softens at its glass transition, typically 60 °C: its HDT is 55 °C under 0.45 MPa and 50⁠–⁠54 °C under 1.8 MPa. As published, Vicat softening is typically 60 °C and melting typically 164 °C.
