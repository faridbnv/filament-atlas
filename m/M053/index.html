PA12-CF is a carbon-fibre-filled polyamide 12. Makers sell it for manufacturing tools and industrial parts that need strength, stiffness and dimensional stability, and they stress low warpage. It is semicrystalline, so it carries light load well above its glass transition: its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 150 °C. On the H2C some products need a hardened nozzle, and makers call for dry storage and drying at 65⁠–⁠110 °C, 4⁠–⁠16 h.

Partly documented Tensile strength (Z) and price rest on 1 product each, notched Charpy impact on 3 products and tensile modulus (XY) on 4 products. Each figure is the median of the products that publish it comparably, and beside it the middle half of them; a value on a test bar no document describes as printed, or with no stated test load, is set apart, except a heat deflection of a plastic that cannot crystallise, which its glass transition sets, unless its document points to a moulded or resin bar.

Among the materials

Against PC-CF, PA12-CF is 17 % weaker flat and 7 % stiffer, and its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 23 °C higher. Against Nylon-CF, maker-undisclosed polyamide, PA12-CF is 85 % stiffer and 41 % stronger flat, and its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 5 °C higher. Among the materials, PA12-CF has a high tensile modulus (XY), notched Charpy impact and price, and a low tensile strength (Z) and density: each is in the top or bottom quarter of the materials that publish it. The rest of its key properties sit in the middle half.

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PA12-CF typically reaches a tensile strength of 58 MPa at a modulus of 4.99 GPa, and breaks at 9.35 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 90 MPa at a flexural modulus of 3.6 GPa.

Thermal properties

PA12-CF's HDT is typically 150 °C under 0.45 MPa and 150 °C under 1.8 MPa, with its glass transition typically at 55 °C. Semicrystalline, it carries light load well above its glass transition. As published, Vicat softening is typically 170 °C and melting typically 171 °C.

After printing

Three products publish values measured after annealing, on schedules from 80 °C for 24 h to 100 °C.

  • Moisture conditioning refers to allowing the print to absorb moisture. This is inevitble since nylons are hygroscopic, but you can expedite the process by keeping the print in a high humidity climate, or submerging them in water, for 48 hours. All of our test specimens were annealed at 100˚C for 16 hours, and then immerged in water at 60˚C for 48 hours. The average moisture content of specimens is 2.57%. Polymaker FIBERON PA12 CF10, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
  • After annealing, max. 1.0 % dimensional shrinkage could be observed in Z-axis depending on infill and layer height, no obvious dimensional shrinkage in XY-axis. Raise3D Industrial PA12 CF, data sheet, p. 2
