PLA Aero is a foaming, lightweight polylactic acid. Makers sell it for RC planes, drones, gliders and figurines, and describe a matt surface. It is amorphous as printed, so it softens at its glass transition: its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 53 °C. On the H2C it prints at 195⁠–⁠250 °C, and makers say the foaming depends on the printing temperature.

Partly documented Price rests on 1 product, tensile strength (Z) and notched Charpy impact on 3 products each and HDT at 0.45 MPa on 4 products. Each figure is the median of the products that publish it comparably, and beside it the middle half of them; a value on a test bar no document describes as printed, or with no stated test load, is set apart, except a heat deflection of a plastic that cannot crystallise, which its glass transition sets, unless its document points to a moulded or resin bar.

Among the materials

Against ASA Aero, PLA Aero is 2.1 times as tough in notched impact, 2.0 times as strong flat and 90 % stiffer. Among the materials, PLA Aero has a low tensile modulus (XY), tensile strength (XY), tensile strength (Z), notched Charpy impact, HDT at 0.45 MPa and density: each is in the lowest quarter of the materials that publish it. Its elongation at break (XY) and price sit in the middle half.

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PLA Aero typically reaches a tensile strength of 20 MPa at a modulus of 1.35 GPa, and breaks at 8.8 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 45 MPa at a flexural modulus of 1.96 GPa.

Thermal properties

PLA Aero's HDT is typically 53 °C under 0.45 MPa and 48⁠–⁠50 °C under 1.8 MPa, with its glass transition typically at 59 °C. As published, Vicat softening is typically 55 °C and melting typically 155 °C.

What makers warn about

  • Using PLA LW requires some printing experience Flashforge PLA LW, data sheet, p. 1
