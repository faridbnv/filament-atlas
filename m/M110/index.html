PCTG-GF is a glass-fibre-filled PCTG copolyester. A maker sells it for tooling, equipment and manufacturing aids, and its makers stress its low shrinkage. Its glass transition is 88 °C. On the H2C it prints at 250⁠–⁠270 °C without chamber heat, but makers call for a ruby or hardened steel nozzle and for drying before printing.

What makers say about printing it

Moisture sensitivity

  • PCTG is moderately hygroscopic and therefore it is recommended to pre-dry the filament at 75°C for approximately 24 hours before usage for optimal results. FormFutura AthenaX GF10, data sheet, p. 2
  • For optimal print results we recommend to print AthenaX GF10 filament from a drying box to avoid that the material can accumulate humidity from the environment. FormFutura AthenaX GF10, data sheet, p. 2
  • Dry box not necessary Spectrum PCTG GF10, data sheet, p. 1

Nozzle wear

  • We recommend to use ruby nozzles or hardened steel nozzles. FormFutura AthenaX GF10, data sheet, p. 1
  • In general these glass fibers will accelerate the nozzle-wear of brass nozzles, much faster than unfilled filaments. FormFutura AthenaX GF10, data sheet, p. 1
  • Ruby or hardened nozzle recommended Spectrum PCTG GF10, data sheet, p. 1
  • Please be aware that glass fiber reinforced filaments contain a relatively high concentration of extremely hard glass fibers, which have an abrasive nature. FormFutura AthenaX GF10, data sheet, p. 1
More from makers: warping and shrinkage and adhesion between layers

Warping and shrinkage

  • Its low shrinkage factor and perfect layer adhesion make AthenaX GF10 a breeze to print with. FormFutura AthenaX GF10, data sheet, p. 1
