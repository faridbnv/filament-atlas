PLA is polylactic acid. Makers sell it for prototypes and display models, and Prusa's Knowledge Base rates it easy to print. It is amorphous as printed, so it softens at its glass transition: its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 56 °C, and the heavier load gives the same. On the H2C it needs no heated chamber and, except a few filled products, no hardened nozzle. Makers' statements on acids and alkalis mostly say it does not resist them.

Among the materials

Against PLA-CF, PLA is 18 % less expensive, 16 % stronger across the layers and 11 % less stiff. Against PETG, PLA is 47 % stiffer and 28 % more expensive, and its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 14 °C lower. Among the materials, PLA has a low HDT at 0.45 MPa and price: both are in the lowest quarter of the materials that publish them. The rest of its key properties sit in the middle half.

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PLA typically reaches a tensile strength of 45.5 MPa at a modulus of 2.5 GPa, and breaks at 6.76 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 76.3 MPa at a flexural modulus of 2.9 GPa.

Thermal properties

PLA is amorphous as printed, so it softens at its glass transition, typically 59 °C: its HDT is 56 °C under 0.45 MPa and 56 °C under 1.8 MPa. As published, Vicat softening is typically 60 °C and melting typically 163 °C.
