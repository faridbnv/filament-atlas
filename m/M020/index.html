PETG is glycol-modified polyethylene terephthalate. Makers sell it for functional prototypes, fixtures and outdoor accessories. Its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 70 °C, below its glass transition. On the H2C it prints at 230⁠–⁠255 °C with a standard nozzle and no heated chamber, and most products call for drying.

Well documented 127 documents from 34 publishers, 96 of them data sheets. Each figure is the median of the products that publish it comparably, and beside it the middle half of them; a value on a test bar no document describes as printed, or with no stated test load, is set apart, except a heat deflection of a plastic that cannot crystallise, which its glass transition sets, unless its document points to a moulded or resin bar. Among the materials

Against PETG-CF, PETG is 54 % less stiff, 37 % less expensive and 32 % less tough in notched impact. Against PETG-GF, PETG is 27 % less stiff and 17 % stronger across the layers. Among the materials, PETG has a low tensile modulus (XY), notched Charpy impact, HDT at 0.45 MPa and price: each is in the lowest quarter of the materials that publish it. The rest of its key properties sit in the middle half.

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PETG typically reaches a tensile strength of 47.3 MPa at a modulus of 1.7 GPa, and breaks at 8.96 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 70 MPa at a flexural modulus of 1.95 GPa.

Thermal properties

PETG's HDT is typically 70 °C under 0.45 MPa and 65 °C under 1.8 MPa, with its glass transition typically at 80 °C. As published, Vicat softening is typically 78 °C.

What makers say about printing it

Printing advice

  • Appropriate selection of the height allows to minimize the effect of thread pulling and polymer accumulation around the nozzle. Prusa Research Buddy3D PET-G, data sheet, p. 2

Warping and shrinkage

  • Shrinkage (ISO 294-4, 2577) 0.2-0.5 % Fabru Type G polyethylene terephthalate (PETG), data sheet, p. 1

On PETG in general

  • Glossy appearance and low warping; fine detail and support release are less favourable than PLA. Finish / detail after Prusa Research, PETG | Prusa Knowledge Base
  • Tune cooling and retraction for stringing and detail; cooling can trade against interlayer bonding. Cooling / stringing after Prusa Research, PETG | Prusa Knowledge Base
More from makers: moisture sensitivity, odour and emissions, adhesion between layers, nozzle wear and supports and removal

Moisture sensitivity

  • PET-G may require drying before use, although usually it is not necessary. Prusa Research Buddy3D PET-G, data sheet, p. 2

Odour and emissions

  • Print in a ventilated room Nanovia PETG, data sheet, p. 1

Adhesion between layers

  • Ideal extruder temp is 260 to 280°C, or higher depending on your printer / thermocouple set-up – some users report requiring temps up to 290°C to obtain optimal layer bonding. 3DXTECH 3DXPRO LG PETG, maker's product page or guide, p. 1

Nozzle wear

  • Ruby or hardened nozzle not necessary Spectrum PET-G Premium, data sheet, p. 1

Supports and removal

  • PVA is the recommended support material for PETG, although SR-30 can also be used when working with the 2XA extruder. UltiMaker MakerBot PETG, data sheet, p. 1

Density and price

PETG's density is typically 1,270 kg/m³, the middle half 1,260⁠–⁠1,290 kg/m³, across 61 products. PETG typically costs 26 CAD/kg, the middle half 25⁠–⁠32 CAD/kg, across the 11 products with a regular price in the sample; the lowest is eSUN PETG Basic at 20 CAD/kg.
