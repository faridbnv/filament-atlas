PVA is polyvinyl alcohol, a water-soluble support material. A maker sells it as support for complex multi-extrusion printing, and Prusa's knowledge base calls it very hygroscopic. On the H2C it prints at 200⁠–⁠205 °C and makers advise drying at 45⁠–⁠80 °C, 8⁠–⁠12 h. A maker warns that it deteriorates or crystallises if it waits too long in the hot support nozzle.

Partly documented Tensile modulus (XY) rests on 1 product, tensile strength (XY) and HDT at 0.45 MPa on 2 products each, price on 3 products, tensile strength (Z) is not published and notched Charpy impact is published only on test bars no document describes as printed. Each figure is the median of the products that publish it comparably, and beside it the middle half of them; a value on a test bar no document describes as printed, or with no stated test load, is set apart, except a heat deflection of a plastic that cannot crystallise, which its glass transition sets, unless its document points to a moulded or resin bar.

Among the materials

Against PLA, PVA is 3.6 times as expensive. Against PETG, PVA is 4.7 times as expensive and 7 % weaker flat, and its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 15 °C lower. Among the materials, PVA has a high elongation at break (XY) and a low tensile modulus (XY) and HDT at 0.45 MPa: each is in the top or bottom quarter of the materials that publish it. The rest of its key properties sit in the middle half.

What makers say about printing it

Supports and removal

  • It is recommended to set the distance between the support and the model to 0, Slow down and turn off the fan for the first layer to bond well with the main material. eSUN PVA, data sheet, p. 2

Moisture sensitivity

  • It is recommended to work with eBOX(3D filament dryer) in printing. eSUN PVA, data sheet, p. 2

On PVA in general

  • Very hygroscopic; moisture control and drying are essential. Moisture after Prusa Research, Water soluble (BVOH/PVA) | Prusa Knowledge Base
More from makers: printing advice, odour and emissions and adhesion between layers

Printing advice

  • You need to print PolyDissolve™ S1 very slowly - we recommend no higher than 60mm/s or 8mm^3/s max volumetric speed. Polymaker PolyDissolve S1, maker's product page or guide, p. 1

Odour and emissions

  • AquaSolve™ PVA has a limited smell during printing and is non-toxic and biodegradable once dissolved in water. FormFutura AquaSolve - PVA, data sheet, p. 1

Adhesion between layers

  • Good bonding to PETG-based materials and other (thermo)plastics that print within a similar temperature range FormFutura Atlas Support, maker's product page or guide, p. 1

Uses, limitations and chemicals

What it is sold for

  • Atlas Support is a water-soluble PVA type of 3D printer filament that is ideal as support material for complex multi-extrusion 3D printing with PETG based filaments as build material. FormFutura Atlas Support, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
  • Improved thermal stability, resulting in less risk on deterioration/crystalizing inside the nozzle FormFutura Atlas Support, maker's product page or guide, p. 1

What makers warn about

  • The printing temperature should not exceed 225˚C for over 5 min. Raise3D Premium PVA+, data sheet, p. 2
  • Though it is possible to use PolyDissolve™ S1 with a single nozzle, it is not recommended due to increase chances of clogging when swapping materials. Polymaker PolyDissolve S1, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
  • Printing with PVA materials does require a certain level of 3D printing expertise as PVA cannot stay too long in the hot secondary/support nozzle and needs to flow and by that needs expert tweaking to find the optimal “waiting temperature” for the material not to deteriorate/crystalizing in the hot secondary/support nozzle when not being 3D printed. FormFutura Atlas Support, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
