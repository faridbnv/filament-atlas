PA6-CF is carbon-fibre-filled PA6, a semicrystalline polyamide. Makers sell it for fixtures, clamps and structural parts used in dry environments. Its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 193 °C, well above its glass transition. On the H2C every product needs a hardened nozzle, Bambu Lab's guide requires an enclosure, and most products call for drying at 65⁠–⁠110 °C, 4⁠–⁠12 h.

Partly documented Tensile strength (Z) and notched Charpy impact rest on 1 product each and tensile modulus (XY), HDT at 0.45 MPa and price on 4 products each. Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PA6-CF typically reaches a tensile strength of 63 MPa at a modulus of 4.16 GPa, and breaks at 8.9 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 140 MPa at a flexural modulus of 4.57 GPa.

Thermal properties

PA6-CF's HDT is typically 193 °C under 0.45 MPa and 164⁠–⁠173 °C under 1.8 MPa, with its glass transition typically at 67 °C. Semicrystalline, it carries light load well above its glass transition. As published, Vicat softening is typically 193 °C and melting typically 219 °C.

Uses, limitations and chemicals

What it is sold for

  • Similar to other Bambu CF Filaments, these outstanding mechanical properties make it an ideal choice for printing fixtures, clamps, automotive and mechanical components and various structural and engineering parts that used in dry environments. Bambu Lab PA6-CF, data sheet, p. 1
  • With excellent heat resistance up to 185°C and outstanding mechanical properties, it combines high strength, high rigidity, and excellent high-temperature resistance, making it suitable for printing products such as automotive structural components and sports equipment. Eryone PA6-CF, data sheet, p. 1
  • This FFF filament is ideal for mechanical parts which require shock absorbing capabilities and good thermal resistance up to 100 °C. Nanovia PA-6 CF, data sheet, p. 1

What makers warn about

  • DuraPro PA6 CF offers notably higher impact resistance than DuraPro PA12 CF, including no-break performance in unnotched testing. However, it absorbs significantly more moisture (9% vs. 1.6% at saturation), meaning it requires more careful storage and drying to maintain performance. DuraPro PA6 CF is also not electrically insulating, unlike DuraPro PA12, due to its carbon fiber content. Extrudr DURAPRO PA6 CF, maker's product page or guide, p.1
