PETG-GF is a glass-fibre-filled PETG copolyester. Makers sell it for structural components and outdoor parts, and stress its dimensional stability and low warping. Its glass transition is 75 °C. On the H2C it prints at 250⁠–⁠270 °C, but makers call for a hardened steel or reinforced nozzle.

Partly documented HDT at 0.45 MPa and price rest on 1 product each, tensile strength (Z) on 2 products, tensile modulus (XY) and tensile strength (XY) on 3 products each and notched Charpy impact is not published. Among the materials

Against PETG, PETG-GF is 37 % stiffer and 14 % weaker across the layers. Against PETG-CF, PETG-GF is 37 % less stiff, 9 % weaker flat and 7 % weaker across the layers. Among the materials, PETG-GF has a high density and a low elongation at break (XY) and price: each is in the top or bottom quarter of the materials that publish it. The rest of its key properties sit in the middle half.

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PETG-GF typically reaches a tensile strength of 45 MPa at a modulus of 2.33 GPa, and breaks at 2.95 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 78 MPa at a flexural modulus of 2.74 GPa.

Uses, limitations and chemicals

What it is sold for

  • By combining glass fibres, anti-UV additives and PETG, natively waterproof and resistant to temperatures up to 80 °C, Nanovia PETG GF UV is the perfect filament for parts subjected to outdoor conditions. Nanovia PETG GF UV, data sheet, p. 1
  • It is particularly suitable for manufacturing structural components or outdoor models that require high impact resistance and durability against drops. Eryone PETG-GF, data sheet, p. 1
