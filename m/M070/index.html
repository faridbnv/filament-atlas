PPA-CF is carbon-fibre-filled polyphthalamide. Makers sell it for automotive, aerospace, electronics, drone and robot parts. It is semicrystalline, so it carries light load well above its glass transition: its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 227 °C. On the H2C it prints at 285⁠–⁠310 °C, every product needs a hardened nozzle, and some chamber windows reach above the H2C's heated-chamber limit.

Partly documented Tensile strength (Z), notched Charpy impact and price rest on 1 product each and tensile modulus (XY), tensile strength (XY) and HDT at 0.45 MPa on 4 products each. Among the materials

Against PPA-GF, PPA-CF is 25 % stiffer and 8 % stronger flat, and its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 75 °C higher. Against PA6-CF, PPA-CF is 97 % stiffer and 71 % stronger flat, and its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 35 °C higher. Among the materials, PPA-CF has a high tensile modulus (XY), tensile strength (XY), tensile strength (Z), HDT at 0.45 MPa and price, and a low elongation at break (XY): each is in the top or bottom quarter of the materials that publish it. Its notched Charpy impact and density sit in the middle half.

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PPA-CF typically reaches a tensile strength of 108 MPa at a modulus of 8.2 GPa, and breaks at 3.2 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 208 MPa at a flexural modulus of 7.89 GPa.

Thermal properties

PPA-CF's HDT is typically 227 °C under 0.45 MPa and 196 °C under 1.8 MPa, with its glass transition typically at 83 °C. Semicrystalline, it carries light load well above its glass transition. As published, Vicat softening is typically 232 °C and melting typically 239 °C.

What it is sold for

  • PPA-CF offers strength approaching metal levels, being 48% stronger and 102% stiffer than normal PA6-CF. This filament excels in mechanical properties, making it ideal for high-performance engineering applications.
