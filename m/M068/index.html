PET-GF is a glass-fibre-filled polyethylene terephthalate. A maker sells it for functional prototypes, jigs, fixtures, automotive parts, brackets and enclosures. Its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 82 °C, and across the layers it keeps 48 % of its flat tensile strength. On the H2C its products all need a hardened nozzle, and makers call for dry storage and advise drying.

Partly documented Tensile strength (XY), notched Charpy impact and HDT at 0.45 MPa rest on 1 product each, price on 2 products and tensile modulus (XY) and tensile strength (Z) on 3 products each. Each figure is the median of the products that publish it comparably, and beside it the middle half of them; a value on a test bar no document describes as printed, or with no stated test load, is set apart, except a heat deflection of a plastic that cannot crystallise, which its glass transition sets, unless its document points to a moulded or resin bar. Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PET-GF typically reaches a tensile strength of 76 MPa at a modulus of 4.13 GPa, and breaks at 5.5 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 132 MPa at a flexural modulus of 4.5 GPa.

Thermal properties

Its products put HDT at 82 °C under 0.45 MPa and 72 °C under 1.8 MPa, against a glass transition of 60 °C. As published, Vicat softening is 233 °C, from one product and melting typically 241 °C.

After printing

Three products publish values measured after annealing, on schedules from 100 °C for 8 h to 120 °C for 16 h.

  • It is recommended to use Fiberon™ PET-GF15 directly without annealing in cases where temperature resistance of PET-GF15 above 80°C is not required. Polymaker Fiberon PET-GF15, data sheet, p. 1
  • After printing, it is recommended to anneal the printed model in an oven at 80-100°C for 8-12 hours. Raise3D Industrial PET GF, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
  • After the printing, the printed part can be annealed to further improve the strength of QIDI PET-GF print part. QIDI PET-GF, data sheet, p. 5

What makers say about printing it

Printing advice

  • After the printing process, it is recommended to dry the model in the oven at 120-130°C for 6-8 hours to increase the strength of the model. Flashforge PET-GF, data sheet, p. 3

Nozzle wear

  • Nozzle & Gear Material Hardened steel Flashforge PET-GF, data sheet, p. 2
More from makers: supports and removal, warping and shrinkage, moisture sensitivity and odour and emissions

Supports and removal

  • If PET GF is used as the support material for itself, please remove the support structure after annealing. Raise3D Industrial PET GF, data sheet, p. 2

Warping and shrinkage

  • For thin-walled areas with a wall thickness of less than 4mm, you can try to add reinforcing ribs or increase the wall thickness to avoid possible annealing deformation. Polymaker Fiberon PET-GF15, data sheet, p. 1

Moisture sensitivity

  • Fiberon™ PET-GF15 is sensitive to moisture and should always be stored and used under dry conditions (relative humidity below 20%). Polymaker Fiberon PET-GF15, data sheet, p. 1

Odour and emissions

  • Odor / Almost odorless Raise3D Industrial PET GF, data sheet, p. 1

Uses, limitations and chemicals

What it is sold for

  • Ideal for functional prototypes, jigs, fixtures, automotive parts, brackets, and enclosures. Polymaker Fiberon PET-GF15, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
  • An ideal choice with multi-colour for reliable printing of fibreglass composites for strong and cost-effective end-use applications. Raise3D Industrial PET GF, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
Finish and accuracy
  • The color of the printing parts will become slightly lighter after annealing due to the change in crystallinity, which is a normal phenomenon. Polymaker Fiberon PET-GF15, data sheet, p. 1
  • In addition, PET GF provides multiple color choices. Raise3D Industrial PET GF, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
