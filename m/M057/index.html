PA6/66 is a polyamide 6/66 copolymer, a nylon. Makers sell it for gears and for engineering and industrial parts. It is semicrystalline, so it carries light load well above its glass transition: its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 130 °C. On the H2C it prints at 250⁠–⁠270 °C, and every product calls for drying.

Partly documented Tensile strength (Z) and notched Charpy impact rest on 1 product each, tensile modulus (XY) and HDT at 0.45 MPa on 2 products each and tensile strength (XY) and price on 3 products each. Each figure is the median of the products that publish it comparably, and beside it the middle half of them; a value on a test bar no document describes as printed, or with no stated test load, is set apart, except a heat deflection of a plastic that cannot crystallise, which its glass transition sets, unless its document points to a moulded or resin bar.

Among the materials

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PA6/66 typically reaches a tensile strength of 61 MPa at a modulus of 2.34 GPa, and breaks at 15 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 91 MPa at a flexural modulus of 2.31 GPa.

Thermal properties

Its products put HDT at 119⁠–⁠140 °C under 0.45 MPa and 70 °C under 1.8 MPa, with its glass transition typically at 67 °C. Semicrystalline, it carries light load well above its glass transition. As published, Vicat softening is typically 158 °C and melting typically 190 °C.

What makers warn about

  • Moisture conditioning refers to allowing the print to absorb moisture. This is inevitble since nylons are hygroscopic, but you can expedite the process by keeping the print in a high humidity climate, or submerging them in water, for 48 hours. All of our test specimens were annealed at 100˚C for 16 hours, and then immerged in water at 60˚C for 48 hours. The average moisture content of specimens is 2.57%. Polymaker PolyMide CoPA, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
