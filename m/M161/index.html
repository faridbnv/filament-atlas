TPU 95A class is thermoplastic polyurethane, a flexible elastomer. Makers sell it for phone cases, insoles and functional parts. Its tensile modulus (XY) is 0.027 GPa, and printed across the layers it keeps 67 % of its flat tensile strength. On the H2C it needs no heated chamber, and nearly every product calls for drying.

Partly documented Notched Charpy impact rests on 1 product, price on 3 products and tensile strength (Z) on 4 products. Each figure is the median of the products that publish it comparably, and beside it the middle half of them; a value on a test bar no document describes as printed, or with no stated test load, is set apart, except a heat deflection of a plastic that cannot crystallise, which its glass transition sets, unless its document points to a moulded or resin bar.

Among the materials

Against TPU-CF, TPU 95A class is 71 % less expensive. Against TPU 85A class and softer, TPU 95A class is 2.2 times as strong across the layers, 2.1 times as stiff and 41 % stronger flat. Among the materials, TPU 95A class has a high elongation at break (XY) and notched Charpy impact, and a low tensile modulus (XY), tensile strength (XY), tensile strength (Z) and price: each is in the top or bottom quarter of the materials that publish it. Its density sits in the middle half.

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), TPU 95A class typically reaches a tensile strength of 31 MPa at a modulus of 27 MPa, and breaks at 499 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 4.26 MPa at a flexural modulus of 87.6 MPa.
