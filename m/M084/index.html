PP-GF is glass-fibre-reinforced polypropylene, a semicrystalline polymer that carries light load well above its glass transition. Its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 144 °C. On the H2C a maker states that a hardened nozzle is necessary, and a maker recommends a brim to improve the adhesion of edges and corners.

Among the materials

Against PP-CF, PP-GF is 57 % less stiff and 27 % less tough in notched impact. Against PA12-CF, PP-GF is 63 % less tough in notched impact, 47 % less stiff and 28 % weaker flat. Among the materials, PP-GF has a low elongation at break (XY) and density: both are in the lowest quarter of the materials that publish them. The rest of its key properties sit in the middle half.

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PP-GF typically reaches a tensile strength of 41.7 MPa at a modulus of 2.63 GPa, and breaks at 3.05 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 80.9 MPa at a flexural modulus of 3.51 GPa.

Thermal properties

Its products put HDT at 139⁠–⁠148 °C under 0.45 MPa and 117 °C under 1.8 MPa, against a glass transition of -5 °C. Semicrystalline, it carries light load well above its glass transition. As published, Vicat softening is 138–164 °C, from one product and melting 158 °C, from one product.

Uses, limitations and chemicals

Finish and accuracy
  • PP can be postprocessed with sandpaper, razor blades, and a variety of other instruments. Wet sanding is recommended (due to airborne particles) but not necessary as the material doesn’t deform under the heat caused by friction.
