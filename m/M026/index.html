PETG-ESD is an anti-static PETG copolyester. Makers sell it for electronics and semiconductor uses where electrostatic discharge matters. It is amorphous as printed, so it softens at its glass transition: its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 75 °C. On the H2C it prints at 250⁠–⁠260 °C, and a maker warns that nozzle temperature and print orientation strongly affect surface resistivity.

Partly documented Notched Charpy impact rests on 1 product and tensile strength (Z) and price on 2 products each. Each figure is the median of the products that publish it comparably, and beside it the middle half of them; a value on a test bar no document describes as printed, or with no stated test load, is set apart, except a heat deflection of a plastic that cannot crystallise, which its glass transition sets, unless its document points to a moulded or resin bar.

Among the materials

Against PETG-CF, PETG-ESD is 3.4 times as expensive, 46 % less stiff and 30 % weaker across the layers. Against PETG-GF, PETG-ESD is 25 % weaker across the layers, 15 % less stiff and 12 % weaker flat. Among the materials, PETG-ESD has a high price and a low tensile strength (Z), notched Charpy impact and HDT at 0.45 MPa: each is in the top or bottom quarter of the materials that publish it. The rest of its key properties sit in the middle half.

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PETG-ESD typically reaches a tensile strength of 39.5 MPa at a modulus of 1.98 GPa, and breaks at 7.3 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 64 MPa at a flexural modulus of 1.72 GPa.

Thermal properties

PETG-ESD is amorphous as printed, so it softens at its glass transition, typically 80 °C: its HDT is 75 °C under 0.45 MPa and 72 °C under 1.8 MPa. As published, Vicat softening is typically 86 °C.

What makers say about printing it

Moisture sensitivity

  • As the ESD PETG filament absorbs moisture easily, it should be dried before being used. Flashforge ESD PETG, data sheet, p. 3

Printing advice

  • Due to dispersed CNTs (Carbon Nano Tubes) in PETG ESD, we recommend to print model with higher infill density and minimal wall thickness of 1.5 mm for a better interlayer bonding quality and ESD property. Raise3D Industrial PETG ESD, data sheet, p. 2

Uses, limitations and chemicals

What it is sold for

  • Its thermal resistance up to 80°C allows allows for the intergration of potentially warm electronics such as motherboards. Nanovia PETG ESD, data sheet, p. 1
  • PolyMax™ PETG-ESD offers electrostatic discharge (ESD) safety with improved toughness making it a good candidate for applications in electronics industry. Polymaker PETG ESD, data sheet, p. 2
  • It is an ideal filament for applications in automotive, aerospace, semiconductor industry, electronic and electrical industries. Flashforge ESD PETG, data sheet, p. 1

What makers warn about

  • Nozzle temperature and print orientation strongly affect surface resistivity. Higher nozzle temperatures lower resistivity; validate the finished part for the intended ESD requirement. Polymaker PETG ESD, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
  • The ESD (Electrostatic Discharge) safety of PETG-ESD filament can degrade over time, depending on environmental factors like humidity, temperature, and exposure to dust or contaminants. PETG-ESD filaments contain conductive additives, which can wear off or lose effectiveness due to surface abrasion or environmental wear. Typically, the ESD-safe properties should remain intact for 1-2 years under controlled conditions, but regular testing is recommended to ensure continued ESD performance. Storing parts in a clean, dry environment can help maintain their ESD properties longer. Polymaker PETG ESD, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
Finish and accuracy
  • For the ESD properties to be active, Nanovia PETG ESD parts should not be covered with paints. Nanovia PETG ESD, data sheet, p. 1
  • Surface finish semi-matte Spectrum petg esd, data sheet, p. 1
